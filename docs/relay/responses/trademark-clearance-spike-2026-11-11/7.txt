What it does #what-it-does
Give it a brand name, an owner name, or no search term at all and just filters, and it returns matching trademarks from every register you selected, with the full public record attached: who owns the mark and where they are based, which IP firm represents them, what goods and services it covers in the language you ask for, which territories it is protected in, whether anyone opposed it and on what grounds, and when it expires.
Both search fields accept a list , so one run can cover many brands or many owners at once. Results are merged and de-duplicated across searches and across offices. Fields with no value at the publishing office are omitted, never guessed.
Large pulls are handled for you: when a filter combination matches more marks than a single query can return, the actor splits it into smaller office and filing-date slices automatically and keeps going, so maxTrademarks in the hundreds of thousands still works.

Open the actor, paste this into the input, and press Run. It returns up to 100 EU trademarks in Nice class 9 filed during 2025, each with the owner's postal address and the goods and services wording in English.
{

Leave both search fields empty to browse a whole register purely by filter. All filters combine with AND; multi-value fields match any of the listed values.

Use cases #use-cases
Global clearance searches. Run one fuzzy search for a proposed brand across all 77 offices and see every conflicting mark, its classes and its owner, before you file.
Portfolio and competitor mapping. Pull an owner's entire worldwide filing history in a single run, including marks filed under local subsidiary names.
Renewal and expiry pipelines. Filter by expiry window and office to get marks coming up for renewal, with the responsible IP firm and its address already attached.
IP firm business development. Find owners filing into your jurisdiction and see who currently represents them, with postal addresses for outreach.
Brand protection and watch services. Monitor new filings in your Nice classes and territories with a filing-date window, and catch lookalikes early with fuzzy matching.
Opposition and litigation research. Study which grounds succeed in a given office and class, and which earlier marks are relied on.

Billing and limits #billing-and-limits

result	0.015	Once per trademark written to the dataset.

FAQ and troubleshooting #faq-and-troubleshooting
Which trademarks are covered? Marks published by the 77 offices listed above, including applications, registrations, expired and ended marks. Each office decides how far back its own history goes.
How fresh is the data? It is read live from the public registers on each run, and every record carries observedAt .
Why do some rows have partyDetailsWithheld ? A handful of offices publish owner and representative details only through a channel that refuses automated requests. Those rows still carry the owner name that the register itself returns, and the flag tells you the postal address was unavailable rather than absent. Owner addresses are available for most European and Latin American offices.
Why is goodsAndServices sometimes not in English? Many offices publish the wording only in their own language. The actor returns your requested language when it exists, then English, then whatever the office published. goodsServicesLanguages tells you which versions exist.
Can I search several brands at once? Yes. markTexts and applicantNames both accept lists, and results are merged and de-duplicated by the mark's global identifier.
What is st13 ? A 13-character identifier that is stable and unique across every office, so you can key on it when you re-run and diff.
Why are applicationDate and registrationDate sometimes inconsistent? A few offices record the registration date of an earlier related right. The dates are reported exactly as the office publishes them, never adjusted.
Can I get more than the count I set? No. maxTrademarks is a hard cap; the actor stops once it is reached.
Do I need an API key? No. The registers are public. Residential proxies are used by default because the registers throttle bulk traffic, and Apify Proxy is already included in your plan.
