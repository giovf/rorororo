Review the questions we ask when you request production access

To access the live (production) environment, you must ensure your software conforms to our terms of use. The terms of use explain what we expect from you when creating and operating software services that use HMRC APIs hosted on the HMRC Developer Hub.

The terms of use apply to all users of the Developer Hub and cover all publicly accessible content, including your software, websites and social media. HMRC may carry out spot checks and retrospective assurance activity, to confirm ongoing compliance.

The terms of use is not a legal relationship between HMRC and any software developer.

HMRC staff will review your responses and information is kept private.

We reserve the right to remove or revoke your access to the Developer Hub and its APIs temporarily or permanently. Persistent non‑compliance with the Developer Hub terms of use may also result in the rejection of further production credential requests.

You have six months to complete your request for production credentials. After that, your progress is deleted and you will need to start again.

About your organisation

Organisation details
Question Notes
Provide details for a responsible individual in your organisation.

The responsible individual:

  • ensures your software conforms to the terms of use
  • understands the consequences of not conforming to the terms of use (opens in a new tab)
What is your organisation’s URL? For example https://example.com.
Provide evidence that your organisation is officially registered.

If you are in the UK, you can identify yourself or your organisation using your:

  • Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR)
  • VAT registration number
  • PAYE reference
  • Corporation Tax UTR

Or you can use your company registration number given to you by Companies House.

If your organisation is outside the UK, we’ll need evidence that your organisation is officially registered in your country.

Marketing your software
Question Notes
Do you use HMRC logos in your software, marketing or website? We will check if you are using HMRC logos in your software, marketing or website.

What this means:
You must not use HMRC logos or other crown copyright material and branding in any content, including your software application, website and marketing material.

Do adverts in your software comply with UK standards?

Advertising that appears in your software (including third party advertising) must follow:

  • Advertising Standards Authority Codes (opens in a new tab)
  • UK marketing and advertising laws (opens in a new tab)

What this means:
You are responsible for all marketing and promotional content produced by, or on behalf of your organisation, including websites, software, blogs, adverts and social media. Your advertising and marketing content must not:

  • mislead customers about what your software can do
  • claim to be ready, compatible or integrated, before access to the live environment is granted
  • present planned features as if they are available now
  • include content that contradicts HMRC’s official guidance and policy
  • use search engine optimisation (SEO) techniques or bulk generated content to promote inaccurate content.
Do you advertise your software as ‘HMRC recognised’? Only use ‘HMRC recognised’ when advertising your software. Do not use terms like ‘accredited’ or ‘approved’.

What this means:

  • You may describe your software as “HMRC recognised” if it has successfully completed HMRC’s formal recognition process. You must not claim or imply your software is ‘HMRC recognised’ unless you have successfully completed the formal recognition process.
  • You must not claim or imply that your software is HMRC approved, accredited, certified, Kitemarked or similar.
Do you get your customers’ consent before sharing their personal data for marketing? You must not share customers’ personal data without their consent. Read the Direct Marketing Guidance (opens in a new tab) from the Information Commissioner’s Office.

About your processes

Development practices
Question Notes
Do your development practices follow our guidance? You must develop your software following our development practices (opens in a new tab).

What this means:
Your software must be built and tested to meet HMRC’s technical specifications before you request live access. Make sure you understand the specific requirements of the APIs you want to integrate with, as each HMRC service has its own rules and documentation.

Does your error handling meet our specification? We will check for evidence that you comply with our error handling specification (opens in a new tab).

What this means:
Your software must be built to meet our error handling specification.

Does your software meet accessibility standards? Web-based software must meet level AA of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) (opens in a new tab). Desktop software should follow equivalent offline standards.

What this means:
Web-based software must meet Level AA of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG). Desktop software should follow equivalent offline standards. To be listed as accessible on HMRC’s Gov.UK software recognition pages, you will need to provide evidence of compliance to this standard.

Service management practices
Question Notes
Do you provide a way for your customers or third parties to tell you about a security risk or incident? We expect you to provide an easy contact method in the case of a security breach.

What this means:
You must provide a way for customers or third parties to report security risks or incidents to you. This should be easy to find and use, enabling issues to be raised and acted on quickly.

Do you have a process for notifying HMRC in the case of a security breach? Any issues concerning the security of personal or customer data must be reported immediately to HMRC by logging a ticket (opens in new tab) within 72 hours, providing a breach contact name and telephone number. You must also notify the ICO about personal data breaches (opens in a new tab) within 72 hours of becoming aware of it.

What this means:
You must have a clear process in place to report security incidents to HMRC. Any issues affecting customer data must be reported immediately, and full details must be provided to HMRC within 72 hours. Where personal data is involved, you must also notify the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach.

Handling personal data
Question Notes
Do you comply with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR)?

To be UK GDPR compliant you must keep customer data safe. This includes telling customers:

  • what personal data you will be processing and why
  • that you are responsible for protecting their data
  • your lawful basis (opens in a new tab) for processing personal data

What this means (UK GDPR compliance):
You must handle customer data in line with UK GDPR. This means being clear with customers about what data you collect, why you collect it, your legal basis for doing so, and your responsibility to keep it secure.

Do you encrypt all customer data that you handle? You must encrypt access tokens and personally identifiable data when it is stored and in transit. Read the UK GDPR guidelines on encryption (opens in a new tab).

What this means:
When storing and transferring customer data, you must encrypt it. This includes access tokens and any information that could identify an individual.

Do you ensure that each customer’s data cannot be accessed by unauthorised users? Read the National Cyber Security Centre’s guidance on keeping user data separate and best practice for username and password security (opens in a new tab).

What this means:
You must ensure that customer data is kept separate and secure. Your system should be designed so that one customer cannot access another customer’s data.

Do you have access control for employees using customer data? Using a personnel security policy and Role Based Access Control (RBAC) will ensure that employees can only access data essential to their job role. Read the National Cyber Security Centre’s guidance (opens in a new tab).

What this means:
Where appropriate, you must implement clear roles and access controls, to ensure employees can only access information relevant to their job.

Can customers get their data from your software if they switch providers? You must allow customers to change, export or delete their data if they want to. Read the UK GDPR guidelines on individuals rights (opens in a new tab).

What this means:
You must allow customers to access, change, export or delete their data if they choose to move to another provider or no longer use your service.

Do you store your customers’ HMRC sign in details? Implementing OAuth 2.0 means there is no need to store HMRC sign in details.

What this means:
You should not store customers’ HMRC sign-in details. Using OAuth 2.0 means customers authenticate directly with HMRC, so your software does not need to collect, handle or store these credentials. Storing HMRC sign-in details creates an unnecessary security risk.

Software security

If you provide software as a service (Saas), we will ask you these questions about your software security.

Software security
Question Notes
Has your application passed software penetration testing? Use either penetration test tools or an independent third party supplier. For penetration testing methodologies read the National Cyber Security Centre Penetration Guide (opens in a new tab).

What this means:
You must test your software for security vulnerabilities before going live. This should include regular penetration testing using appropriate tools to identify and fix vulnerabilities that could put the customer or HMRC at risk.

Do you audit security controls to ensure you comply with data protection law? Assess your compliance using the ICO information security checklist (opens in a new tab).

What this means:
You must regularly check that your security controls are working and comply with data protection regulations. This includes reviewing how you protect customer data and taking action where gaps or weaknesses are identified.

Fraud prevention data

If you use VAT (MTD) or Income Tax Self Assessment (MTD) APIs, we will ask you these questions about fraud prevention data.

Fraud prevention data
Question Notes
Does your software submit fraud prevention data? You must submit header data in line with the fraud prevention specification (opens in a new tab).

What this means:
You must include HMRC‑specified fraud‑prevention header data in all API requests, as this helps protect customer accounts and identify suspicious activity.

Have you checked that your software submits fraud prevention data correctly? Before you submit any header data, you need to use the Test Fraud Prevention Headers API (opens in a new tab).

What this means:
You must test your fraud prevention headers before going live to make sure your software sends the required security data correctly and to avoid your production access request being rejected or delayed.

Customers authorising your software
Question Notes
Where are your servers that process customer information? For cloud software, check the server location with your cloud provider.

What this means (server location):
You must know where customer data is processed and stored. If you use a cloud provider, you should confirm the server location and make sure it meets your data protection responsibilities.

Do you have a privacy policy URL for your software? You need a privacy policy covering the software you request production credentials for.

What this means:
Commercial software must have a clear and accessible privacy policy that explains how you collect, use and protect customer data. It must accurately reflect how your product works and be specific to the service you are providing, rather than generic or template wording.

Do you have a terms and conditions URL for your software? You need terms and conditions covering the software you request production credentials for.

What this means:
You must have clear and accessible terms and conditions for your software that sets out how your service works and the responsibilities of both you and your customers.
