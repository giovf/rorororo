How it works

From a name to a decision you can defend.

The final call always stays with your team. Verifex supplies the proof; it does not make your legal judgement. Exact-only never earns a compliance clearance: a quick exact-name lookup is never a full check.

  1. Step 1

    Screen at onboarding

    A name goes in from your app, a file or the dashboard. Verifex checks it against every list on your plan.

  2. Step 2

    Every list accounted for

    You see which lists answered and how current they are. A missing list means review, never a silent pass.

  3. Step 3

    Review only what matters

    Possible matches arrive with the reason they matched, so analysts close false alarms fast and escalate real ones.

  4. Step 4

    Keep the proof, stay current

    Every decision is saved for audit. When a list changes, your existing customers are checked again.

Questions buyers ask.

Something else? Talk to us.

What is Verifex?+

Verifex is a sanctions, PEP and AML screening API with a decision record behind every result. Screening checks people and companies against the sources you choose. Verifex Shield monitors transactions for fraud and AML risk and screens the counterparty in the same call. Both keep the evidence of what was checked, so a result can be explained to an auditor later.

What does Verifex screen against?+

Verifex screens people and companies against sanctions lists, PEP data and watchlists, including OFAC, UK Sanctions, the United Nations, BIS and multilateral debarment lists. 46 sources are catalogued. Coverage and update frequency vary by source; the data sources page shows the live state of each one.

Is there a free plan?+

Yes. The Free plan includes 50 screens a month with no credit card, so you can test the API and read real responses before you pay. Paid plans add more screens, more sources, evidence export and webhooks.

What is Verifex Shield?+

Shield is fraud and AML transaction monitoring. Each transaction gets one of five outcomes (clear, review, delay, escalate or block) with the rules that fired, a score, and a signed evidence record. Compliance teams can tune a rule, test it on past decisions and deploy it without engineering work. Shield is generally available and is switched on per account by Verifex, so talk to us to enable it.

Can we move from SanctionScanner to Verifex?+

Yes. Shield offers a SanctionScanner-compatible API with the same paths, fields and response envelope, plus Shield's own extensions. Most integrations change the base URL and the API key. A native API is available for new integrations.

Does a no-match result mean a customer is clear?+

Not on its own. A result is only eligible for your clearance path when standard mode ran, every in-scope source answered, the candidate set was complete and the evidence record is available. If any of those fails, Verifex holds the result for review and says why. The legal decision stays with your team.

Is a PEP the same as a sanctioned person?+

No. A sanctioned person is someone you are legally barred from dealing with. A PEP (politically exposed person) is someone in public office, or close to one, who needs extra checks, not a ban. Verifex keeps the two apart: a PEP result never becomes a sanctions-grade result by itself.

What does it mean that a decision can be replayed?+

Every screening records the data, matching evidence, source snapshot, and engine version behind the result, tied to a decision hash. That lets your team reconstruct and verify what was recorded (the versions, decision identity, and source provenance of a past decision) instead of relying on a screenshot or the final label alone.

What do the benchmark numbers mean?+

They come from a benchmark of the deployed engine (Production Engine vNext, measured August 12, 2026) against a corpus of 1,325,118 listed rows across 53 sources as it stood on that date. Exact-name recall was 97.33% (95% CI 94.83–98.64%), all 300 sanctions positives were actionable, and none of the 150 PEP-only cases were served at sanctions-grade severity. This is a self-administered vendor benchmark, not an independent audit. The deployed engine has changed since that measurement, so these figures describe the configuration tested on that date and not current behaviour; a new measurement is pending.
