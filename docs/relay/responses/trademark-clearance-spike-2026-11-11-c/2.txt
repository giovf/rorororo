The UK register by the numbers
2.95 million trade marks sit on the UK register, but only 53% are live registrations. The rest are dead, expired, removed or withdrawn, so a search hit is not automatically a live obstacle.
40.5% are figurative (logo) marks; the remainder are word marks.
The single most-filed class is Class 9 (/trademark-classes/class-9) (software and electronics), followed by Class 35 (/trademark-classes/class-35) (advertising and retail) and Class 41 (/trademark-classes/class-41) (education and entertainment).
1.18 million distinct owners hold UK marks, and 67% of them hold just one.
Computed by Trademark Dashboard from 2,950,119 UK IPO register records, July 2026.
How to read a UK trademark register entry
What the register does not tell you: the UK trade mark database shows every registered and pending mark, but it does not tell you whether a mark is in genuine use, whether a confusingly similar (not identical) name already exists, or whether your own name is safe to use. Under the Trade Marks (Relative Grounds) Order 2007 the IPO will not refuse a new mark over an earlier similar one; it only flags it and notifies the earlier owner. The record shows filings, not your risk. Find every mark a company owns (/find-uk-trademark-by-owner) , or see how a clearance search differs (/guides/trademark-search-vs-clearance-uk) .
What Is in the UK Trademark Database?
The UK trademark register is the official record of every trade mark application and registration in the United Kingdom. Maintained by the UK Intellectual Property Office, it has been in continuous operation since the Trade Marks Registration Act 1875 — making it one of the oldest trademark registers in the world.

History of the UK Trademark Register
The UK was one of the first countries to establish a formal trademark registration system. Key milestones include:
1875
Trade Marks Registration Act
Parliament establishes the first UK trademark register, enabling businesses to formally register and protect their marks. The Bass Brewery’s red triangle becomes registration number 1 on 1 January 1876.
1938
Trade Marks Act 1938
A major overhaul introducing the concept of “Part A” and “Part B” registrations and strengthening owner protections. This Act governed UK trademark law for nearly six decades.
1994
Trade Marks Act 1994
Current governing legislation, aligning UK law with the EU Trade Marks Directive. Introduces the Nice Classification system and modernises registration procedures.
2021
Post-Brexit Register
Following EU exit, all EU Trade Marks designating the UK are converted to equivalent UK registrations. The register grows by approximately 1.4 million “comparable” marks.
2026
Today
The register contains 3 million records, receives 70,000–90,000 new applications annually, and is fully searchable online. TrademarkDashboard adds free exact and word-match search plus attorney cross-referencing on top of the official data, with AI similarity matching in the paid clearance report and Pro.
How the UK Trademark Data Is Structured
Nice Classification
Every mark is filed in one or more of 45 Nice classes (/trademark-classes) . The most popular classes in the UK are Class 25 (/trademark-classes/class-25) (clothing), Class 35 (/trademark-classes/class-35) (business services), and Class 9 (/trademark-classes/class-9) (software & electronics). Filing costs increase per class (£205 first class, £60 each additional).
Status Lifecycle
A mark progresses through distinct stages: Filed → Examined → Published for Opposition → Registered . Marks can be refused at examination or opposed by third parties during the publication period. Registrations last 10 years and can be renewed indefinitely.
Owners & Attorneys
Each registration records the owner (individual or company) and the attorney or agent who manages it. Our attorney directory (/attorneys) ranks 3,200+ UK trademark attorneys by filing volume, enabling you to compare firms and find specialists in your industry.
Open Data & Licensing
UK IPO register data is published under the OGL v3.0 and the Re-use of Public Sector Information Regulations 2015. This means anyone can access, use, and redistribute the data for commercial or non-commercial purposes with attribution.

The UK operates its own national register, separate from European and international systems. Understanding which register applies to your business is essential.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the UK trademark database?
The UK trademark database is the official register maintained by the UK Intellectual Property Office (IPO). It contains records for every trade mark application filed in the UK since 1876 — 3 million records including active, expired, and refused applications. The data is publicly available under the Open Government Licence.
How many trademarks are registered in the UK?
3 million records as of 2026, including approximately 1.1 million active registrations. The IPO receives 70,000–90,000 new applications per year. TrademarkDashboard indexes the complete register with daily updates.
Is the UK trademark database free to access?
Yes. The underlying data is public information under the OGL v3.0. You can search for free via the IPO's own tool or TrademarkDashboard. Both free tools match exact and word text, but TrademarkDashboard adds owner and class views, daily updates, and US-register cross-search in one place. AI similarity and look-alike matching is included in the paid clearance report (from £9.99) and Pro.
How often is the database updated?
TrademarkDashboard synchronises daily. New applications, status changes, renewals, and cancellations are reflected within 24 hours of the IPO publishing them.
Can I download the full UK trademark database?
The IPO publishes bulk data under OGL. The raw files require significant processing. TrademarkDashboard provides a pre-processed, searchable interface with class analysis and owner lookups built in free. AI similarity matching is part of the paid clearance report and Pro.
What data fields does each record contain?
Mark text or image description, registration number, application and registration dates, Nice class(es), current status, owner name and address, and the attorney or agent of record.
What is the oldest UK trademark?
The Bass red triangle, registered on 1 January 1876 under the Trade Marks Registration Act 1875. It is widely considered the UK's first registered trademark (registration number 1).
How does the UK register relate to EUIPO and WIPO?
The UK register covers the United Kingdom only. EUIPO manages EU-wide marks (no longer covering the UK post-Brexit). WIPO manages the Madrid System for international marks, which can designate the UK. TrademarkDashboard searches UK, EU, and international registers.
