About EUIPO
What is EUIPO?
European Union Intellectual Property Office, Alicante, Spain.
The European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO) is the EU agency responsible for the European Union Trade Mark (EUTM) and Registered Community Design systems. Founded in 1994 as the Office for Harmonization in the Internal Market (OHIM) and renamed EUIPO in 2016, it is headquartered in Alicante, Spain, and operates under EU Regulation 2017/1001.
A single EUTM application gives unitary protection across all 27 EU member states, one filing, one fee, one renewal, one register. EUIPO receives roughly 196,000 EUTM applications per year and maintains over 1.3M active registrations alongside historical filings dating back to 1996. There is no use requirement to file or register, but a mark becomes vulnerable to revocation if it has not been put to genuine use within five years.
EUIPO publishes trademark data through TMview (a multi-office search front-end) and its eSearch Plus REST API. Signa ingests the eSearch Plus feed, including EUTMs and Madrid Protocol designations of the EU, normalizing 18 EUIPO status codes into a unified status object, resolving co-applicants, and enriching each record with the goods/services descriptions, classification events, publications, and opposition/cancellation proceedings exposed by the detail endpoint.
What makes it different
Unitary right, one EUTM registration covers all 27 EU member states with a single application, fee, and renewal
No use requirement to register, unlike USPTO, you can file and register without proving commercial use (genuine use becomes relevant only after 5 years)
11 mark types, word, figurative, shape, position, pattern, colour, sound, motion, multimedia, hologram, and "other"
Fast Track examination, qualifying applications (pre-approved goods/services from the harmonized database) can publish in roughly half the standard time
File in any of 23 official EU languages plus one of 5 working languages (EN/FR/DE/IT/ES) for the procedure
Seniority claims, preserve the priority date of an earlier national mark when consolidating into a single EUTM
SME Fund, EU SMEs can reclaim up to 75% of EUTM application fees (capped at €1,000) through annual EUIPO grants
Opposition system, third parties have a 3-month window after publication to oppose before registration issues

FAQ
EUIPO API questions
Is the EUIPO eSearch Plus API free?
Yes, eSearch Plus is a free public REST API offered by EUIPO. Access requires registering an application on the EUIPO developer portal (dev.euipo.europa.eu), subscribing to the Trademark Search product, and completing an identity verification step (passport or national ID plus proof of address). Once approved, you authenticate with OAuth2 client credentials. The data itself is public; you build the ingestion and normalization pipeline. Signa is paid because it does that work for you.
How do I get an EUIPO API key?
For EUIPO direct: register at dev.euipo.europa.eu, create an app, subscribe to the Trademark Search API, submit identity documents to docs.apiplatform@euipo.europa.eu, and wait for approval. After approval you get a client ID and client secret used in an OAuth2 client_credentials flow against https://euipo.europa.eu/cas-server-webapp/oidc/accessToken. For Signa: sign up at app.signa.so, copy your API key, and pass it as Authorization: Bearer. The same key works for EUIPO and every other office Signa covers.
What is the difference between eSearch Plus and TMview?
TMview is a multi-office search front-end run by EUIPO that aggregates trademark data from EUIPO, WIPO, and dozens of national offices into a single human-facing search UI. It is meant for end-user lookups, not programmatic ingestion. eSearch Plus is the EUIPO-only REST API that returns structured JSON for EUTMs and Madrid designations of the EU, it is what you build on. Signa ingests eSearch Plus directly and separately consumes other offices through their own native feeds rather than relaying through TMview.
What are the EUIPO API rate limits?
The eSearch Plus API enforces approximately 200 requests per minute per client. Search responses lack several detail fields (markText, full applicants array, goods/services descriptions, events, proceedings), so a full ingestion typically combines a fast search pass with a slower detail pass, and the detail pass becomes the bottleneck. OAuth2 tokens expire every 2 hours and must be refreshed. Signa does not pass these limits through: we share the underlying EUIPO quota across customers and surface our own per-key rate limits.
How fresh is EUIPO data on Signa?
EUIPO has an active daily sync at 07:00 UTC that uses the RSQL `updateDate>=watermark` filter to pick up both new EU direct filings and updates to Madrid Protocol designations of the EU. New and changed records are normalized, enriched with detail-endpoint data (goods/services, events, proceedings), and indexed within minutes. Every record carries an updated_at timestamp so you can verify freshness inside the data itself.
Can I bulk export EUIPO trademark data via Signa?
Yes. Signa exposes a cursor-paginated search endpoint that streams the full EUIPO register, including both EUTMs and Madrid designations of the EU. For very large exports, enterprise plans include S3 delivery of the same normalized records as gzipped NDJSON, refreshed daily.
Does the EUIPO API include national trademarks from EU member states?
No. EUIPO covers EUTMs and Madrid Protocol designations of the European Union. Both grant unitary protection across all 27 member states through the EUIPO register. National registrations in France (INPI), Germany (DPMA), Spain (OEPM), and other member-state offices are separate filings on separate registers. Signa has the national registers of France (INPI) and Sweden (PRV) today, and Germany, Spain and Italy are on the roadmap. Madrid designations of individual EU countries are searchable through the same Signa endpoint via WIPO.
How does Signa handle EUIPO opposition and cancellation data?
EUIPO exposes proceedings (oppositions, cancellations, appeals, invalidations) through the detail endpoint of eSearch Plus. Signa ingests the full proceeding record, kind, parties, status, key dates, and decision events, and normalizes EUIPO status strings like OPPOSITION_PENDING, CANCELLATION_PENDING, APPEALABLE, and REMOVED_FROM_REGISTER into the canonical status object (status.primary / status.stage). All 18 EUIPO status codes are mapped, and decision events (opposition_decided, cancellation_decided, appeal_decided) are emitted to the event timeline so you can reconstruct the prosecution and post-grant history of any mark.
