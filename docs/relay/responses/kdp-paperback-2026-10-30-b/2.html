How to Save Tax 2026/27

By Nick Braun PhD

This comprehensive annual tax planning guide explains how to pay less income tax, national insurance, capital gains tax and inheritance tax. It is essential reading for anyone who wants to know about the latest tax changes, including salary earners, business owners, landlords and investors.

Crammed full of tax saving ideas, How to Save Tax is written in plain English and contains lots of easy to follow examples.

With 150 pages of tax planning tips, this guide comes FREE with any purchase from Taxcafe. The latest edition includes all major tax announcements made in the November 2025 Budget and is completely up to date.

What Information is Contained in the Guide?

How to Save Tax covers a broad range of topics including:

  • All tax changes announced in the November 2025 Budget plus all other important tax changes made in recent times.
  • Recent changes to all the major taxes:
    • Income tax
    • National insurance
    • Capital gains tax
    • Inheritance tax
    • Corporation tax
    • Stamp duty land tax
  • Capital gains tax saving tips
  • Inheritance tax saving tips
  • How to pay less income tax and national insurance, with special chapters for:
    • Salary earners
    • The self employed
    • Landlords and property investors
    • Company directors
  • How to split your income with your spouse or partner and save thousands in tax.
  • How to minimise the child benefit charge.
  • Full details of the Scottish income tax regime.
  • How to obtain more tax relief on your pension contributions.
  • A whole chapter covering tax exemptions and amounts that are completely tax free.
  • A chapter covering all the recent tax changes affecting individuals.
  • A chapter covering all the latest business tax changes.

About the Author

Dr Nick Braun founded Taxcafe in 1999. Since then the company has become one of the UK's best-known tax publishers and has won several prestigious business awards. Nick has been a specialist tax writer since 1989, first in South Africa where he edited the monthly Tax Breaks publication and since 1999 in the UK, where he has authored several tax books. Nick also has a PhD in economics from the University of Glasgow where he was awarded the prestigious William Glen scholarship and later became a Research Fellow.
