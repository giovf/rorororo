Use Play app signing
With Play app signing, Google manages and protects your app's signing key. This article explains how to set up Play app signing for new or existing apps, register signing-key fingerprints with API providers and manage key upgrades and upload-key resets. To configure Play app signing, you must have admin permission.
Google protects your app's signing key on the same secure infrastructure that it uses to store its own keys. These keys are protected by Google's key management service (KMS). To learn more about this industry-leading infrastructure, read the Google Cloud security white paper (https://cloud.google.com/security/overview/whitepaper) .
Why use Play app signing?
Beyond safeguarding your keys against permanent loss or compromise, enrolling in Play app signing unlocks enhancements for your Android App Bundles ( .aab ). By letting Google sign the optimised distribution APKs generated from your app bundles, you gain access to valuable services like automatic protection (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10183279) and automatic strings translation (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844778) using Gemini models.
Games gain access to additional automatic services like Play Games Sidekick (https://developer.android.com/games/pgs/play-games-sidekick) , Play as you download (https://developer.android.com/google/play/play-as-you-download) and free-of-charge trials for paid titles (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16923846)
Core concepts
When you use Play app signing, you deal with two distinct keys. Understanding the difference – and their technical formats – is critical to avoiding authentication issues with third-party APIs.
Key type
Who holds it?
Technical details and purpose
Upload key
You (keep this secure!)
Format: Stored in a Java keystore ( .jks or .keystore ).
Requirement: Must be an RSA key of 2048 bits or more.
Purpose: You use this key to sign your app bundle before uploading it to the Play Console. Google uses it to verify your identity. If compromised or lost, Google can reset it for you.
App signing key
Google Play
Format: Associated with a public certificate ( .der or .pem ).
Requirement: Google-generated keys are RSA 4096-bit. Custom keys must be RSA 2048-bit or higher.
Purpose: Google uses this key to sign the final APKs delivered to users' devices. You can have Google generate this key, or you can provide your own. This key cannot be reset if you manage it yourself and lose it.
Quantum ready
Google Play
Format: Two keys associated with public certificates ( .der or .pem ).
Requirement: Quantum-ready hybrid signing combines a classical RSA 4096-bit key and a post-quantum ML-DSA-65 key. Google generates a new classical key for the hybrid signature that differs from the classical key that it uses for devices running Android versions lower than 17, resulting in your app using three distinct keys.
Purpose: Devices running Android 17 or higher verify the app using APK signature scheme v3.2 (https://source.android.com/docs/security/features/apksigning/v3-2) to protect it against future quantum computing attacks. Devices running older Android versions continue verifying the app with the classical signature blocks.
Note: For maximum security, your upload key and app signing key should be different.
When Google generates and signs your APKs with the app signing key, it uses apksigner to add two stamps to your app's manifest: com.android.stamp.source and com.android.stamp.type . These stamps ensure that your APKs are securely traceable to the original signer.
Set up Play app signing
The setup process depends on whether you are publishing a new app or migrating an existing one.
For new apps
Create a new app. Your app will be automatically enrolled in quantum-ready, hybrid signing with Google-generated keys. We also generate a separate classical key to be used in signature blocks recognised on devices running Android 16 or lower.
Create an upload key. Generate a keystore to sign your release app bundle. You can generate it in Android Studio (https://developer.android.com/studio/publish/app-signing#generate-key) or use the Java keytool utility from the command line (https://developer.android.com/build/building-cmdline#sign_cmdline) .
Upload your app bundle. In Play Console, prepare a new release. By default, when you upload your app bundle, your app is automatically enrolled in quantum-ready, hybrid signing with Google-generated keys.
Optional: Change your app signing key. Advanced developers who want to manage their own key can change this default before a release is rolled out in the open testing or production track. Go to the Play app signing page ( Protected with Play (https://play.google.com/console/u/0/developers/app/protect-with-play) > Play Store protection > Manage Play app signing ) and click Change key . You can then choose to:
Use the same key as another app in this developer account.
Provide a copy of your app signing key by following the instructions.
For existing apps
If you manage your own keys and upload APKs, you can upgrade to Play app signing to take advantage of app bundles and Play enhancements.
Sign in to Play Console, select your app and go to the Play app signing page ( Protected with Play (https://play.google.com/console/u/0/developers/app/protect-with-play) > Play Store protection > Manage Play app signing ).
Accept the Terms of Service if you haven't already.
Transfer a copy of your original key. Download the PEPK tool and follow the step-by-step instructions to encrypt and upload your existing app signing key from any repository.
Creating a new upload key is recommended. Generate a new key in Android Studio to use as your upload key going forwards, and register its certificate in Play Console.
Register with API providers
If your app uses APIs such as Google Maps, OAuth or Facebook Login, those services authenticate your app using your app signing key's fingerprint.
Because Google signs the final APK, you must register the Google-held app signing key fingerprint with your API providers, not just your local upload key.
Sign in to Play Console, select your app and go to the Play app signing page ( Protected with Play (https://play.google.com/console/u/0/developers/app/protect-with-play) > Play Store protection > Manage Play app signing ).
Scroll to the App signing key section.
Copy the required fingerprints (SHA-1 or SHA-256). If your app uses quantum-ready hybrid signing, you must copy the fingerprints for three keys and register each of them with your API providers: your new classical and PQC keys used on newer devices and your classical key used on older devices.
Paste these fingerprints into your API provider's console (for example, Google Cloud console).
Tip: Update your assetlinks.json file with these fingerprints if you use Android App Links.
Manage your keys
Upgrade your app signing key
If your app signing key is compromised, or you need a cryptographically stronger key, you can request an annual key upgrade for all installs on Android 17 (API level 37) or higher.
How enforcement works across Android versions
Android 17 (API level 37) or higher: The Android platform strictly enforces the use of your upgraded quantum-ready hybrid key. See APK signature scheme v3.2 (https://source.android.com/docs/security/features/apksigning/v3-2) .
Android 13 (API level 33) to Android 16 (API level 36): The Android platform strictly enforces the use of your latest classical key. See APK signature scheme v3.1 (https://source.android.com/docs/security/features/apksigning/v3-1)
Android 7 (API level 24) to Android 12 (API level 32): The Android platform itself does not enforce the upgraded key and still recognises the latest classical key. However, Google Play Protect provides additional validation by checking that app updates are signed with your latest classical key, unless the user turns it off.
Important: Because the platform does not enforce the upgraded key on Android 12 (API level 32) or lower, if you use the same key across multiple apps to share data, those older Android versions will only recognise the legacy key for features such as custom permission sharing.
Upgrade your key
Sign in to Play Console, select your app and go to the Play app signing page ( Protected with Play (https://play.google.com/console/u/0/developers/app/protect-with-play) > Play Store protection > Manage Play app signing ).
Under the 'App signing key' section, click Upgrade key .
Choose your upgrade path:
Let Google Play generate a new app signing key. This is recommended.
Use the same app signing key as another app in this developer account.
Provide a copy of your app signing key by following the instructions.
Click Save .
Register your new key fingerprints with your API providers. If your app uses quantum-ready hybrid signing, you must register the fingerprints for two new keys: your new classical key and your new PQC key.
Request an upload key reset
If you lose your upload key or suspect that it was compromised, you are not locked out of your app. Request an upload key reset by doing the following:
Create a new upload key in Android Studio.
Export the certificate to PEM format:
keytool -export -rfc -keystore upload-keystore.jks -alias upload -file upload_certificate.pem
Note: For help with keytool and keystore paths, see the Android Studio app signing guide (https://developer.android.com/studio/publish/app-signing) ).
In Play Console, go to the Play app signing page ( Protected with Play (https://play.google.com/console/u/0/developers/app/protect-with-play) > Play Store protection > Manage Play app signing ).
In the 'Upload key certificate' section, click Request upload key reset .
Enter the reason for the reset.
Upload your upload_certificate.pem file and click Request .
Best practices and alternative distribution
Protect your account
Protect your Play Console access by enforcing two-step verification for all users.
Apply app bundle enhancements
Enrolling in Play app signing unlocks access to enhancements for your app bundles. To apply enhancements, first make any necessary configuration changes when creating a new release, and then upload a new app bundle.
Distributing outside Google Play
If you distribute through other app stores and want to use the same signing key everywhere, you have two options:
Let Google generate your app signing key, then download a signed, universal APK from Play Console or the Play Developer API to distribute elsewhere. In Play Console, go to the Latest releases and bundles page for your app ( Test and release > Latest releases and bundles ), select your app bundle and select the Downloads tab.
Generate the app signing key that you want to use for all app stores and transfer a copy of it to Google when you configure Play app signing.
Learn more about how app updates work (https://developer.android.com/google/play/app-updates) and your options for enabling or disabling cross-store updates.
Test your app
You can use these testing methods:
Use Internal app sharing (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844679) to test exactly what Google Play will deliver to users.
Download device-specific APKs from the app bundle explorer and install them locally using adb install-multiple *.apk .
APK signature scheme v4
Play app signing automatically uses v4 signing for eligible apps to support optimised distribution on devices running Android 11 or higher. No action is required on your part.
This excludes all apps using quantum-ready hybrid signing, which is not compatible with v4 signing. Read more about the technical benefits in the APK signature scheme v4 documentation (https://developer.android.com/about/versions/11/features#signature-scheme-v4) .
Self-hosted Google Cloud projects
If you have highly specific security requirements, such as using OEM keys, you can use the Play Developer API to enrol in Play app signing using a self-hosted Google Cloud project.
This is a non-standard setup that is not encouraged. Using a self-hosted project means that you assume full responsibility for app signing operations.
It prevents Google Play from performing essential features like disaster recovery.
Google cannot regenerate or fix an existing release. To fix any release-related errors, you must upload a new release.
Advanced optimisations, certain Play enhancements and security features such as quantum-ready hybrid signing may not be available in this configuration.
Generate or import your signing keys into Cloud KMS, ensuring that the location is configured as multi-region US, and follow the KMS key import documentation (https://cloud.google.com/kms/docs/importing-a-key) .
To change your signing key, use the enrollApp method (https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/appsigning/enrollApp) . Signing keys can only be changed before the app is released to open testing or production tracks.
To upgrade your signing key after publishing, use the rotateAppSigningKey method (https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/appsigning/rotateAppSigningKey) .
Grant the following Identity and Access Management (IAM) roles to the client or service account authenticating with the Google Play Developer API:
role/cloudkms.signer (https://cloud.google.com/kms/docs/reference/permissions-and-roles#cloudkms.signer)
roles/cloudkms.viewer (https://cloud.google.com/kms/docs/reference/permissions-and-roles#cloudkms.viewer)
roles/cloudkms.publicKeyViewer (https://cloud.google.com/kms/docs/reference/permissions-and-roles#cloudkms.publicKeyViewer)
Ensure that you have sufficient quota to perform cryptographic operations. In particular, we recommend increasing the quota for asymmetric_requests (https://docs.cloud.google.com/kms/quotas) to at least 350 QPS.
To see how signing keys are used, enable audit logs (https://cloud.google.com/kms/docs/audit-logging) in your Cloud KMS project.
