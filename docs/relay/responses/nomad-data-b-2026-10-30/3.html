FAQs
How can Doc Chat support a claims AI evaluation?

Doc Chat is designed to analyze complete files, answer specific questions, produce structured outputs, and connect findings to source citations. Claims teams can test it on representative files and score both the output and the verification workflow. The goal is to determine whether cited, complete-file analysis can reduce review effort, improve consistency, and make broader audits or screening practical while keeping professional judgment in the process.

Can AI outperform a human benchmark during a claims pilot?

Yes, an AI system can surface a documented fact that an earlier human review missed. That does not mean the AI should be accepted automatically. It means the benchmark and the output should both be checked against the source evidence. A sound pilot includes a discrepancy-resolution process so reviewers can determine whether the system is wrong, the answer sheet needs correction, or the underlying file remains ambiguous.

How should claims teams test AI citations?

Reviewers should confirm that each citation opens the correct document and passage, supports the claim being made, preserves relevant context, and represents conflicting evidence when it exists. Citation presence is not enough. The evidence must be precise and appropriate. Teams should also measure how long verification takes, because a technically correct citation that still requires a long search provides limited workflow value.

What metrics should insurers use to evaluate claims AI?

Insurers should combine technical and operational measures. Useful metrics include material-fact recall, unsupported-statement rate, citation accuracy, correction rate, review time per file, time to completed work product, consistency across repeated tests, user adoption, escalation rate, and the percentage of validated findings that lead to action. The right weighting depends on the use case and the consequences of different errors.

Why is extraction accuracy alone an incomplete measure?

A claims task usually requires more than copying fields. Professionals must understand chronology, distinguish reported statements from documented findings, reconcile conflicts, recognize missing support, and apply the information to a decision or work product. A tool can score well on isolated fields while omitting a consequential event or creating significant verification work. Evaluation should therefore include completeness, analysis, citation quality, workflow effort, and business impact.

What is extraction accuracy in claims AI?

Extraction accuracy measures how often a system correctly identifies and captures information from source documents, such as names, dates, diagnoses, payment amounts, policy terms, or treatment events. It is an important technical measure because downstream analysis depends on reliable source data. However, it does not show whether the system found every material fact, connected evidence across documents, answered the claims question, or produced an output a professional can verify and use.

What role should claims professionals play when using AI?

Claims professionals should define the review requirements, verify material findings, and resolve uncertainty. AI can help organize information and surface evidence, but reviewers remain responsible for interpreting that evidence, applying their expertise, and making the final claims decision.

How can AI fit into an existing claims review workflow?

Evaluate whether the tool produces outputs reviewers can use in their daily work, such as summaries, timelines, and comparisons with supporting citations. Tools such as Doc Chat can be tested against these needs, including how easily reviewers can ask follow-up questions, verify findings, and transfer results into their documentation.

How can insurers measure whether AI saves review time?

Measure the time required to prepare files, generate outputs, verify evidence, correct errors, and document the result. Compare that total with the existing process while assessing review quality. Faster generation alone does not establish a meaningful improvement if reviewers spend the saved time repairing the output.

Why are source citations important in AI-assisted claims review?

Source citations help reviewers verify findings without repeating a full-file search. Each citation should lead to a document, page, or passage that supports the statement in context. Reviewers should also check whether other records qualify or contradict that finding.

Is an accurate summary enough to demonstrate that an AI tool works?

An accurate summary is a useful starting point, but it does not prove that the tool considered every relevant record. Test specific questions and broader tasks separately. Ask the tool to identify changes over time, reconcile conflicting accounts, and flag missing information, then compare its findings with the underlying evidence.

What types of files should an AI pilot include?

Include typical claims alongside difficult cases with long medical histories, poor scans, duplicate records, conflicting statements, and varied document types. Begin with well-understood files that have established summaries and timelines, then expand to cases that were not used to configure the tool.

How should insurers evaluate AI for claims review?

Start with representative claims files and clear acceptance criteria. Compare the AI’s findings with an experienced reviewer’s work, checking for missed facts, unsupported conclusions, incomplete timelines, and conflicting evidence. Measure the full review process, including verification and corrections, to determine whether the tool delivers practical value.

What should healthcare organizations look for in an AI vendor?

Restrictions may be necessary for certain tools and data, but a ban alone may not remove the business need driving employees to use AI. What is shadow AI in insurance?

Shadow AI is employee use of AI tools, features, or accounts outside the organization’s approved technology and governance environment. An example is uploading a claim file to a personal or consumer AI account to create a summary without confirming that the tool and configuration are approved for the information involved.

What are the biggest AI risks in insurance?

What is the difference between a pre-payment and post-payment claims audit?

A pre-payment audit checks a proposed payment before funds are issued. A post-payment audit reviews payments already made and can incorporate later evidence, retroactive offsets, duplicates, or changes in eligibility. Many insurers benefit from using both controls together.

What is a claims payment audit?

A claims payment audit reviews whether an insurance payment is accurate, supported, and consistent with the policy and current claim evidence. It can assess benefit calculations, eligibility, offsets, duplicate payments, payment duration, and required documentation.

Coverage drift is the gradual change in policy protection over time when revisions are not fully recognized during renewal. It may involve new exclusions, revised endorsements, changed conditions, different definitions, altered limits, or modified underlying requirements. Coverage drift can occur even when premium remains stable.
