A payment method fee — also called a surcharge, a convenience fee or a cash-on-delivery fee — is an amount added to an order because of how the customer chose to pay. Pay for Payment sets one per WooCommerce payment method, as a flat amount, a percentage or both, and a negative amount makes it a discount instead.

Pro — conditional fees

Different rules for different orders

A flat fee punishes the wrong orders. $2 on cash on delivery is brutal on a $40 basket and invisible on a $900 one. Stack several rules on one payment method and give each its own conditions — the first rule that matches is the one charged.

Small baskets

when cart total is under $50
charge $2.00 flat

Covers the handling cost on orders too small to absorb a percentage.

Export orders

when country is not CZ
and shipping is not Pickup
charge 3%, max $12

Conditions combine with AND. Leave the list empty and the rule always applies.

Wholesale

when user role is Wholesale
give −2% on bank transfer

A negative amount is a discount. Push customers to the method that costs you least.

Nine things a rule can look at

  • Cart total
  • Coupon applied
  • Product in cart
  • Category in cart
  • Country or state
  • Customer role
  • Shipping method
  • Shipping zone
  • Shipping class

Each one matches or excludes a list; the cart total takes an “at least” or “at most” amount. Coupon codes and shipping methods accept * wildcards, so one rule can cover every SUMMER* coupon.

Order matters

  1. 1Small baskets — under $50$2.00
  2. 2Export orders — outside CZ3%
  3. 3Everything else2.5% + $0.50

Rules are checked top to bottom, and at most one is charged — the first whose conditions match. If none match, there is no fee. Drag to reorder, or leave the last one unconditional as a catch-all.

Frequently asked questions

The ones that come up most. The complete FAQ has all of them, including licences, refunds and renewals. If yours isn't in either, write to me.

Why does a fee already show on the cart page?

Because the cart doesn't know yet which payment method your shopper will pick, so it shows the fee for whichever one is listed first. That fee updates as soon as they choose a method at checkout. Two fixes: put a fee-free method first in your list — works in the free version — or use Pro to hide the fee on the cart page entirely.

Does it work with the block checkout?

Yes, and the total updates live when the shopper changes payment method. One known limitation: the Checkout block has to sit in the page content rather than in a template part for that live update to fire. Every other limit is written down too.

I'm on version 2.x — do I have to reconfigure anything?

No. Your settings migrate themselves the first time 3.0 loads, and the old values stay where they were, so rolling back still works. Every developer filter kept its name and signature — a snippet written years ago still runs.

How does the fee reach my accounting?

As an ordinary WooCommerce fee line, tagged with the gateway it came from and where it was written, so it shows in wp-admin, on customer emails and in exports. If your invoicing plugin ignores fee lines, Pro can write it as a product line instead.

What about orders I create by hand, or a pay-for-order link?

WooCommerce never runs cart fees in either place. Pro writes the fee there itself.

A card payment failed and the customer paid by bank transfer instead — which fee is on the order?

On the free plugin, none: when the customer switches method on the pay-for-order page, the card fee is removed and WooCommerce never runs cart fees there, so nothing replaces it. Nobody is charged for a method they did not use. On Pro, the bank-transfer fee: the old line is replaced by the right one, and the pay-for-order page shows each method's fee and the resulting total before they pay.

Is tax handled correctly?

You choose whether the fee is taxable, which tax class it uses — including the highest class already in the cart — and whether the amount you typed includes tax. VAT-exempt customers are never charged tax on the fee.

What if Pro turns out not to be for me?

You are covered three times over. The free version (opens in a new tab) stays free and keeps working whatever you decide; the 7-day trial takes no card at all; and if you do buy and change your mind, refunds run 14 days, no argument.

What do I need to run it?

PHP 8.0 or newer, WordPress 6.2, WooCommerce 7.7.0. HPOS is declared, WPML translates the fee title, the admin ships in eight languages, and five multi-currency plugins are supported: Price Based on Country, CURCY (Multi Currency for WooCommerce), WCML, FOX (WOOCS), and WooCommerce Multi-Currency when the store currency is USD. The full compatibility list has the versions each one needs.
