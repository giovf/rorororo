CUT MODELS FOR 3D PRINTING
Cut it. Pin it.
Print it.
Ninja Cut splits a model into printable parts and gives every cut a pin and a socket that fit, so the halves line up by themselves when you glue them.
[Ninja Cut cutting the mascot, adding keys and laying the parts on a print bed]
STRAIGHT CUT · FREEHAND · LACE CUT · PINS & SOCKETS · RECESSED SEAM · EXPLODE · HOLES · PRINT BED · STL & 3MF
PRINT WHAT DOES NOT FIT THE BED
A model too big for the printer is just a model that has not been cut yet.
Cutting is the easy half. The hard half is gluing the pieces back in the right place. Ninja Cut does both in the sidebar, without leaving Blender and without a single boolean set up by hand.
01 / CUT
Three ways to cut, one of them your own
Draw Cut Line drags a straight cut across the model, previewed live. Freehand follows the curve you draw, and stays quick even on dense sculpts. Lace Cut is painted on the surface like a knife running round an arm or a leg: turn the view in the middle of the ring and carry it on round the back, and the ring stays where you painted it.
Nothing is cut until you say so. A closed lace ring turns into points that slide over the surface, with an outline on the model showing where the cut will go; a Freehand curve turns into points you drag, add with Ctrl+click or remove with X. Cut, or Enter, confirms. Esc cancels.
[Illustrated cutting workflow with a simulated Cut interface]
Tip Cut a limb with Lace Cut rather than a straight line: the ring follows the shape, so the seam hides in the silhouette.
02 / PINS
A pin and a socket that fit, on every cut
No second step and no modifier stack: the cut already carries its connector. Pick Rectangular , Oval , Dovetail (a rail that slides in) or Magnet (a recess for a magnet in each half) and shape it while you watch: size, thickness, chamfer, taper, depth, left and right, up and down, segments, a line or a ring of pins, socket gap and flip, all live in the viewport.
The pin is drawn as a blue silhouette that shows through the surface, so you see it inside the part while you adjust it. Insert Pin places one more anywhere on a model, and carves its socket into the part above it.
[Illustrated alignment key with a simulated Pins interface]
Tip Socket Gap is the printing clearance, in real millimetres. Raise it when your printer prints tight, lower it when the joint ends up loose.
03 / RECESSED SEAM
A joint that hides inside the model
One part of a straight cut gets a step that follows the outline of the cut, kept in from the edge; the other part gets the matching recess, a little wider and deeper by its own Socket Gap. The walls slant in, so the parts find each other and centre themselves, and the key of the cut stands on top of the step.
It is built into the meshes, without a boolean, and both parts stay closed. It follows any outline, concave, with holes or in several islands, and stays adjustable until you apply the cut.
Depth Offset Taper Segments Own Socket Gap Flip
04 / EXPLODE
See both halves without moving anything
Every cut opens the parts so you can look inside right away. The Distance slider moves them in real time, and Back puts them together exactly where they belong, ready for the next cut. The Active list keeps every cut: confirm it, select its parts, or take it back.
[Ninja mascot parts separated beside a simulated Explode Parts interface]
05 / HOLES & BOOLEANS
Room for magnets and dowels
Place a hole on the surface to preview it where the cursor is, set diameter and depth, then add it. The size starts from the part itself, so you are adjusting a sensible hole instead of typing one from scratch. The Boolean row unites, subtracts and intersects models in one click, and Fit carves one model into another with the printing gap.
Magnets Dowels Live preview Union · Subtract · Intersect · Fit
06 / PRINT
Checked, laid out, exported
Print Bed lists 193 resin printers at their exact build volume, plus the common filament printers and a Custom size, and draws the plate in the viewport with a grid and the build volume. Orient for Print works like a slicer's auto layout: cut faces down, every part centred on the bed, scaled to fit if you want, with the Socket Gap kept at its real size.
Print Check finds thin walls, overhangs and open edges for your kind of printer, and Tolerance Test prints the right gap for it. Export writes one STL per part or one 3MF with every part named, in real millimetres, posed for the printer, with a report of size, volume and layers.
[Ninja parts arranged for printing beside a simulated Status and Export interface]
193 resin printers Orient for Print Print Check Tolerance Test STL per part 3MF
GOOD TO KNOW
Requirements & compatibility
Blender 4.2 or newer. Developed and tested on Blender 5.2.
Nothing else to install: no external libraries, no account, no internet connection.
Install through Edit > Preferences > Add-ons > Install from Disk, with the ZIP from this product.
Lengths follow Scene > Units, so set the scene to millimetres for printing work.
The drawing tools need a 3D viewport, which is where you would use them anyway.
Before you get started
Each cut gets one key, with up to eight pins in a line or a ring; Insert Pin adds more wherever you need them. A Freehand cut has a curved face, so it takes no key or seam of its own: use Insert Pin there. A mesh that is not watertight is still cut and capped, and Ninja Cut tells you about the open edges it found, so you can fix the model before printing.
Ninja Cut is licensed under GPL-3.0-or-later, as every Blender add-on must be.
The still illustrations show simulated interfaces; the animation at the top is recorded in Blender. The mascot is illustrative and is not an included 3D model.
