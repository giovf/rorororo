What it does
Project Box Maker builds a printable enclosure for your electronics project in one click: a rounded box with wall and floor, a lid with a friction-fit lip (optionally held by corner screw posts), standoffs placed from presets for the Arduino Uno, Raspberry Pi 4 and 5, Arduino Nano and ESP32 DevKitC, and port openings (rectangular or round) on any wall. Everything is real geometry in millimetres: the box and the lid are separate, closed (watertight) meshes that you can export as STL or 3MF straight away. And nothing is final: change the size, the board, the lid or any opening later and the box is rebuilt in place.
Who it's for
Makers who design their own cases for Arduino, Raspberry Pi and ESP32 projects, sensor nodes, controllers and small gadgets, and who would rather not open a CAD program for a simple box. Also useful for anyone who needs a quick, correctly sized housing with screw posts and cutouts for a lab, a classroom or a product prototype.
Key features
Rounded box: inside length, width and height, wall, floor, corner radius and corner smoothness are all settings. The box sits on the 3D cursor with its underside at z = 0.
Board presets: Arduino Uno R3 (four M3 standoffs), Raspberry Pi 4 and 5 (four M2.5 standoffs on the 58 x 49 mm pattern), Arduino Nano and ESP32 DevKitC (these boards have no mounting holes, so you get four plain support posts under the corners) and a custom board with your own size and corner holes. The box can be sized to the board plus a clearance you choose, or you set the inside size and the board is centred.
Standoffs with pilot holes for M2, M2.5, M3 or M4 self-tapping screws, with adjustable height.
Lid with friction lip: lid thickness, lip height, lip thickness and the lip clearance (default 0.2 mm) are settings. The lid is its own object, either beside the box for printing (flat side down, lip up) or turned over on the box.
Screw posts: four corner posts with pilot holes, matching clearance holes in the lid and notches in the lip around them.
Port openings: presets for USB-C, Micro-USB, USB-A, Ethernet RJ45, HDMI, Micro-HDMI and a DC barrel jack, plus your own rectangle or round opening. Choose a wall (or the lid), the position and the height. Add as many as you need.
Change it any time (new in 1.1): F9 (Adjust Last Operation) changes every setting of the box or opening you just made. Later, Update Box rebuilds the selected box and its lid in place with new settings (same objects, position, materials and modifiers) and cuts all its openings again. Load Settings puts a box's own settings back into the panel, and the panel warns when they differ.
Openings list: every opening is stored on the box. Edit its side, size or position in the list, or remove it, and the wall is rebuilt without a trace.
Lid openings: round or rectangular holes through the lid plate, for a button, an LED, a display or a cable.
Checks before cutting: an opening that would not fit on the wall, would cut into the floor or the rim, overlaps another opening or leaves the lid's lip ring, screw posts that collide with standoffs, or a board that does not fit are refused with a clear message in the panel, and nothing is changed. After every cut the add-on checks that the mesh is still closed (0 open edges) and that the opening removed the material it should.
Units you can trust: 1 Blender unit = 1 mm by default, so the exported STL has the right size in every slicer. Scene units, 1 cm, 1 m or 1 inch are options (the panel warns when 1 unit is 1 m), and openings keep their size in millimetres even on an object with unapplied scale or rotation.
Undo (Ctrl+Z) and a short report with the sizes after every action.
How to use
Install the zip, press N in the 3D Viewport and open the Box tab.
Pick your Board (or No board and type the inside size), then set wall, lid and screw posts if you want them.
Click Create Box . The box and the lid appear at the 3D cursor.
With the box (or its lid) active, choose a wall or the lid and a port preset (or your own size), set the position and height, and click Add Opening . Repeat for every port.
Need a different size or board after all? Change the panel and click Update Box : the openings are cut again in the new box.
Select the box and the lid and export STL or 3MF for your slicer.
Compatibility
Blender 4.2 LTS or newer, tested on 4.2 LTS and 5.2 LTS. It installs as a Blender extension (Get Extensions > Install from Disk), and a legacy add-on zip is included.
Good to know
The board presets use the published outline and hole positions of each board. The add-on does not know where the connectors of your particular board sit, so you set the position and height of every opening yourself; the panel shows the height of the board's top surface.
The Arduino Nano and ESP32 DevKitC presets give four plain support posts, not pins or screw holes: these boards have no mounting holes. Many ESP32 boards differ in size, so check yours or use the custom board.
The board itself is not modelled, and openings are not placed from a board's connectors: you set each position. Openings can be added to boxes made with Create Box only.
Update Box and removing an opening rebuild the meshes from the settings: changes you made yourself in Edit Mode are replaced (the object, its transform, materials and modifiers stay). Boxes made with version 1.0 have no stored settings: they still take openings, but to change them create them again.
No outside-dimension mode and no vent-slot pattern yet: the sizes you type are inside sizes, and vents are made from several openings.
Openings must lie on the straight part of a wall, above the floor and at least 0.8 mm below the rim. Rounded corners are not cut into.
Fit depends on your printer: 0.2 mm lip clearance is a snug fit on a well-tuned FDM printer; raise it if the lid is too tight. Always print a small test first.
Sizes are set in millimetres in the panel whatever the units setting is. The default 1 Blender unit = 1 mm gives plain numbers in Blender and the right size in the slicer.
