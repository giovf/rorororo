Sunfield builds photorealistic solar power plants and rooftop solar arrays inside Blender. Pick a starting point (a utility tracker farm, fixed-tilt tables on a meadow, a desert bifacial plant, a thin-film field, an agrivoltaic fence, a family house, a warehouse roof or a farm barn) and change anything: rows, modules, mount, tilt, ground, building and condition. Everything rebuilds live in a fraction of a second, even plants with thousands of modules.
Every part is modeled after real plants. Single-axis trackers stand on driven piles with bearings, a slew drive, dampers and module rails, and they turn east to west from one slider or follow the sun over your animation. Fixed tables have front and rear posts, rafters, purlins and clamps. Dual-axis trackers sit on pedestal poles; east-west ridges and vertical bifacial fences cover the dense and agrivoltaic layouts.
Modules carry real cell layouts: half-cut mono cells with busbars, blue polycrystalline cells, all-black panels, frameless thin film and bifacial glass, with dust and age you control. The site gets rolling terrain from Poly Haven ground photos, gravel roads, inverter stations on concrete pads, a chain-link fence with barbed wire and a gate, CCTV poles and a weather station.
Rooftops get gable and hip houses with tiles, slate or metal, flat commercial roofs with ballasted rows or east-west pairs, metal barns, or your own building mesh. Arrays keep clear of chimneys, vents and roof edges like real installers do.
