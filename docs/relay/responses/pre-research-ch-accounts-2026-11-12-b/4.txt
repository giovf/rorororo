Company credit checking explained
What is a company credit check?
A company credit check is an assessment of the financial position and credit risk
associated with a business. It brings together financial, legal and corporate
information so that you can make a more informed decision about whether to trade
with that company and, where appropriate, how much credit to provide.
Credit checking is commonly used before offering payment terms to a new customer,
increasing an existing customer's credit facility, entering into a supplier
agreement or reviewing the financial position of an existing business relationship.
Rather than relying on one piece of information, a company credit report considers
a range of indicators. These can include the company's financial performance,
filing history, age, size, industry, directors, County Court Judgments, mortgages
and charges and other factors associated with insolvency risk.

Understanding the report
What's included in a company credit check?
In addition to public company information supplied through Companies House,
our company credit reports bring together a range of financial and risk
indicators designed to give you a broader view of a company's credit position.
Company Credit Risk Score
Credit risk is a measure of how likely a business is to become insolvent
within the following 12 months. The company is scored out of 100, providing
a straightforward indicator of the level of credit risk associated with
the business.
The score should be considered alongside the information supporting it.
A single numerical score is useful for comparison, but the underlying
financial and company information helps explain why a business has
received its current rating.
Recommended Credit Limit
The recommended credit limit provides a guideline for the amount of credit
that might reasonably be extended to a business at any one time.
This can help businesses establish more consistent payment terms and avoid
exposing themselves unnecessarily to a customer whose financial position
may not support the proposed level of credit.
County Court Judgments
A County Court Judgment can indicate that a creditor has previously needed
to use court proceedings to recover a debt.
Company credit reports can show County Court Judgments (CCJs) registered against a company
during the previous six
years, including the judgment date, court, amount, case number, date paid and current
status.
Company Financials
Financial information filed at Companies House is converted into a format
that makes it easier to review company performance over time.
Depending on the accounts filed by the company, this can include figures
such as cash at bank, net worth, assets, liabilities, working capital,
turnover and other financial measures.
Looking at several years of financial information can provide considerably
more context than viewing the latest accounting period in isolation.
Key Risk Factors
The report identifies factors contributing to the company's current
credit position. These can be positive, neutral or negative and help
explain the reasoning behind the headline risk score.
Director and Secretary History
Current and historic company appointments can provide useful context
about the people responsible for running a business.
The information can include an individual's role, how long they have
been associated with the business and appointments held at other companies.
Shareholders
Shareholder information can help explain who owns or has invested in a
company. Where information is available, reports can include shareholder
names, capital, share values, share classes and percentage ownership.
Company and Group Structure
Information extracted from company filings can help identify where a
business sits within a wider corporate group.
This may include the ultimate holding company and subsidiaries where
those relationships are disclosed in the available company records.
Mortgages and Charges
Charges registered at Companies House can provide information about
borrowing secured against the company or its assets.
Reports can help you review satisfied and outstanding charges by type,
date, status and the organisation to which the debt is owed.
Company Event History
A chronological company history makes it easier to review important
changes since incorporation, including company name changes, director
appointments and Companies House filings.
Risk methodology
How is a company credit score calculated?
Company credit scores are calculated using an algorithm that considers historical
insolvency data and a range of characteristics associated with the company being
assessed.
The variables used in the assessment are adjusted according to factors such as
company size and industry. The scoring model is reassessed periodically so that
the relationship between the available company data and insolvency risk remains
relevant.

Understanding credit limits
How is a company credit limit calculated?
A recommended credit limit is calculated by considering the company's financial
position together with its credit risk rating.
Where a company has a positive credit rating and stronger financial indicators,
such as net worth, working capital, operating cash flow, debtors, cash and turnover,
this may support a higher recommended credit limit.
Where these measures are weaker or negative, the recommended exposure may be
reduced accordingly.
Credit limits are a guide, not a guarantee.
Your own commercial circumstances, payment terms, risk appetite and existing
exposure to the customer should also form part of any credit decision.
Practical use
How to use a company credit report
A company credit report is most useful when the different parts of the report
are considered together rather than treating any one indicator as the answer
to whether a company should receive credit.
Information
What it can tell you
Credit Risk Score
Provides a quick indication of the company's relative insolvency risk.
Credit Limit
Provides a guideline for the level of commercial credit exposure.
Financial History
Shows whether financial strength appears to be improving, stable or declining.
CCJs
Can highlight previous difficulties in paying creditors.
Charges
Provides information about certain forms of secured company borrowing.
Directors
Provides background on the people responsible for managing the company.
Ownership
Helps explain who controls or has invested in the business.
Checking a new customer
Before agreeing credit terms with a new customer, review both the recommended
credit limit and the information supporting the company's risk score. This can
help identify circumstances where shorter payment terms, a lower credit facility
or payment in advance may warrant consideration.
Reviewing existing customers
Credit checking is also useful for existing customers. A customer's financial
position can change over time, particularly where your exposure to that customer
has increased or their payment behaviour has changed.
Comparing potential suppliers
Financial stability can also be relevant when selecting important suppliers.
A financially distressed supplier may create operational risk even where your
business is not extending credit to them.
Keeping information current
How often is company credit information updated?
Company information is updated using daily feeds from official data sources.
When new information becomes available, this can feed through into the company
report and the information used to assess its credit position.
Because company circumstances can change, it is useful to review important
customers periodically rather than relying indefinitely on a credit check
completed when the commercial relationship first began.

Frequently asked questions
Company credit check FAQs
What is a company credit check?
A company credit check reviews financial, corporate and risk information
associated with a business to help assess its creditworthiness and the
risk involved in extending commercial credit.
What does a company credit score mean?
The score provides an indication of the company's credit risk. Company
Check scores businesses out of 100 and uses the available company,
financial and insolvency information to assess the likelihood of
insolvency during the following 12 months.
What is a company credit limit?
A company credit limit is a recommended guideline for the amount of
commercial credit that might be extended to a business at any one time.
It should be considered alongside your own risk policies and commercial
circumstances.
Does a credit check include Companies House information?
Yes. Company credit reports include public company information sourced
from Companies House alongside additional credit risk, financial and
analytical information.
Can a company credit score change?
Yes. A company's score can change when its underlying financial,
corporate or risk information changes. New accounts, filings, CCJs and
other company events may affect the assessment.
How often should I credit check a customer?
There is no single review frequency suitable for every business.
Higher-value relationships, customers receiving substantial credit
and businesses showing signs of financial difficulty may justify
more frequent review.
Can a credit report guarantee that a company will pay?
No. A credit report provides information that can support a commercial
decision, but it cannot guarantee future payment or predict every change
in a company's circumstances.
