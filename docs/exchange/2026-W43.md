### 1. The tax workbook on Etsy — winner (12 × 2 = 24)

- **Buyers with money, on the shelf.** A one-listing UK shop sells an HMRC sole-trader tracker at £7.19: **172 sales and
  18 five-star reviews after one year**; a UK VAT-seller bookkeeping sheet shows **≈ 1,500 reviews**; the Payhip twins of
  the same product sell at £6.95–9.99; the £69 "accountant-verified" sheet sold nothing in four months (spike §3,
  search-index snapshots of 2026-10-23 — Etsy forbids automated reads, so counts are what the index kept).
- **Gap.** ~30 listings per query, most single-listing shops with 0–7 reviews at £3–14; none under £10 combines SA103
  mapping, the MTD quarterly totals (mandatory from April 2026 at £50,000+) and a Trading Allowance check with visible
  sales. The job recurs every tax year.
- **Why it beats the baseline.** It opens a shelf whose buyers are measured (owner rule 09-24), costs half a day with the
  xlsx tooling already in the sandbox and nothing recurring, and is the only candidate this week that can show a sale
  within 30 days of listing (≈ £5.80 net at £7.19; 20 sales a month ≈ £115, more than every launched product has earned
  together). The Meta entry (18) stays in the queue — the slots build both; this decision is where the *new* unit goes.
- **The rule it strains, said plainly.** Etsy charges a one-time, non-refundable set-up fee before a shop opens (US$15 at
  its 2024 introduction, "varies by location", waived in some promotions; 2026 guides say US$15–29). That is **£11–22 plus
  VAT, over ARCHITECTURE's "< £10 to launch"**, and this routine's own list disqualifies "launch cost over £10". So the
  exchange does not spend it or assume it: the venture opens as `idea`, the workbook is built at £0, and the shop opening
  is **the owner's decision in one batched request** (action 029, written by the build that finishes the workbook, with the
  amount Etsy shows at "Total due now"; inside the £47.60 headroom; a `cost` row the day it is charged). The owner's
  identity (Persona ID + selfie, a personal bank account, a card for fees) is the same one-off seller-account shape as
  Figma Community — asked, never assumed (CLAUDE.md). If the owner declines, the file still has a shelf: Payhip (£0 to
  list; three rivals there) is spiked below, and our own Stripe Managed Payments link is the fallback at reach 1.
- **What the operation cannot do here.** Read etsy.com by automation (its Terms forbid it: no relay host, no scanner, no
  metrics job), log in to upload, or answer buyers. The funnel is Etsy's seller emails through inbox triage (a SELF-CAUSED
  row on launch) and the owner's Shop Manager stats; ~15 owner minutes a month at the rival's volume. The listing copy says
  what the buyer gains and what the file is not (record-keeping, not MTD filing software), with a true launch price and a
  dated end — the DMCC fake-urgency rule, as answered on the owner's 10-18 note.
- **Proof / kill.** A first sale within 30 days of listing; ≥ 10 sales in 60 days. 0 sales 60 days after listing with ≥ 5
  views a day → delist, keep the file for the £0 shelves. Day-60 item queued by the build that records the listing.
