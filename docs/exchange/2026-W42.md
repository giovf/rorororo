### 1. x402 Bazaar listing — winner (9)

- **Where the buyers are.** 34,668 resources; the top 200 took 518,867 paid calls from 17,160 payer-slots in
  30 days, at a median price of US$0.003 (`quality.l30DaysTotalCalls`, `l30DaysUniquePayers` on every item).
  Discovery is public and agent-native: `searchX402Resources` by intent, the Bazaar MCP, agentic.market.
  gankdat's x402 lane already exists, is funded by the facilitator's free tier up to 1,000 settlements a
  month, and prices each register at US$0.005 — inside the catalogue's p75.
- **Gap we fill.** There is no UK register, company register, tender feed or sanctions list in the top 200;
  the only compliance-shaped resources (EDGAR 69 calls, an OFAC wallet screen 12) show the category is thin
  but present. gankdat would be the first official-source UK/EU public-data seller in the catalogue, with 17
  endpoints and agent-ready schemas generated from the same zod schemas that build the MCP tools.
- **Why it beats the baseline.** It opens a shelf (owner rule 09-24) where payment is the listing and the
  payer count is public; it costs about a day and no recurring money; it is the only candidate that can turn
  the x402 lane's three-month zero into a reading within 30 days. `actions-minutes` (7) is a cost item.
- **Constraints.** The lane must move from `x402-hono` 1.2 (v1: `maxAmountRequired`) to the v2 packages
  (`@x402/hono` + `@x402/extensions/bazaar`, or the CDP SDK's `createX402Server`) — every indexed resource
  read today is v2, and the docs describe only the v2 declaration (`declareDiscoveryExtension`). Metadata must
  describe, never instruct (content policy). Indexing needs **one settled payment through the CDP Facilitator**,
  which no sandbox or runner can make without a funded buyer wallet: the build raises the owner step when the
  metadata is live (either one `x402-fetch` call from the owner's own wallet, ~5 min, or a US$1 buyer wallet
  as a repository secret so the metrics runner can make and repeat it — the owner's call, ≤ 10 min). The
  self-paid half-cent is the documented onboarding step, not usage: the Daily numbers row must keep counting it
  as internal (`x402_paid` from the probe's client id).
- **Risk.** Sellers report resources passing validation yet never indexed (cdp-sdk issues #806, #759); the
  proof is read from the catalogue (`listX402DiscoveryMerchant` for our `payTo`), never assumed. If the
  Facilitator refuses a v1 settlement meanwhile, nothing is lost: the lane stays as it is.
- **Proof.** Our `payTo` address returns ≥ 17 resources from the discovery API within a week of the settled
  call; ≥ 50 paid calls from ≥ 5 payers (not ours) in the first 30 days (`bazaar:` reading, item 5). Day-30
  read queued for 2026-11-14.

### 2. x402 aggregator MCP — parked (4.0)

The owner's shape exists and sells: stableenrich.dev resells Exa search at US$0.01 and did 19,141 calls for
168 payers in 30 days against Exa's own 5,348 — the reseller outsells the origin. But the economics are
measured too: Exa's listed price is US$0.007, so the gross margin is US$0.003 a call, the Facilitator takes
US$0.001 of it past 1,000 settlements a month, and the best reseller in the catalogue nets about US$38 a
month. The volume is in crypto token data, web-search and people-enrichment resale (the last two needing the
upstream's resale terms and, for people data, a GDPR posture Blind Mode cannot give), not in registers.
Building it means an x402 *client* holding USDC in the Worker (a spending key, the owner's), a catalogue, and
the v2 lane that #1 builds anyway. Trigger: our own listing shows ≥ 50 paid calls/30 d (the shelf converts
for us) — then score an aggregator of the categories agents actually pay for, with each upstream's resale
terms read first. `x402-facilitator-fee` keeps its trigger behind this one.
