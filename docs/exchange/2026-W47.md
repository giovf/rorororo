### 1. Google Play — the owner's own move, taken as a spike (4 × 2 = 8)

- **What happened.** On 11-16 the owner registered a Play Console developer account (US$25, £18.50 — a sixth of the
  capital spent so far), declared trader status and passed the photo-ID check by the next morning. The two things that
  kept Play out of scope since September — the fee and the identity step — were the owner's to spend, and are spent.
- **What the terms say** (read today, `exchange-w47-2026-11-18` 1–2). A personal account created after 13 November 2023
  cannot publish to production until a closed test has held **12 testers opted in for 14 consecutive days**, and the
  production application asks about tester engagement ("fewer than 12 opted-in testers or insufficient tester engagement"
  sends an app back to testing). Organisation accounts are outside that article. Paid apps and in-app sales pay a 15 %
  service fee on the first US$1M. The operation has no testers; the twelve are people the owner would have to find and
  keep opted in — the one cost no routine can pay.
- **What the shelf looks like for the obvious product.** A Play search for "self assessment tax calculator" (GB) returns
  HMRC's own app (4.7 stars), Pie Tax, Taxfix and two dozen free calculators — the tax estimate page as an app would be
  the thirtieth free one. No niche with a paying buyer is identified yet; that, not the build, is the open question.
- **Why a spike and not a park.** The owner's unprompted £18.50 is the strongest statement of intent in the repo since
  the Etsy note, and the question the spike answers is concrete: is there a product this operation can build in a week
  (from what exists: the tax estimate logic, the reseller workbook's profit maths, the mileage and expense logs, the
  register lookups) whose Play niche shows paying rivals with thin competition — and can CI build and sign an Android
  bundle at £0 — so that a request for twelve testers, if one is ever made, asks for something worth the owner's hours.
  Park conditions are written into the item: every candidate niche led by free apps with ≥ 100k installs; no path to an
  app bundle on a hosted runner; or the minimum-functionality policy barring a wrapped web page.
- **Honest expectation.** Both browser extensions sat at 0–5 users on store search alone for six weeks before the owner
  retired them. A Play listing has the same shape. The spike has to find evidence that says otherwise or park the shelf
  with the tester count as its trigger.
