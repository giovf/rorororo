Why a ruler, and why ours

A reading ruler keeps your eyes on one line: the band moves with the mouse, the rest of the page stays as it is. People with ADHD or dyslexia, anyone reading long documents, and anyone presenting on a shared screen use one. The free ruler extensions do this well on ordinary pages. Where they stop is the PDF: the browser's PDF viewer draws pages as pictures, so a ruler extension cannot see the text. ReadFocus takes the other route — "Read this PDF" in the popup fetches the file and rebuilds each page as real paragraphs in ReadFocus's own reader, so the ruler, the bold word starts and the fonts work on it like on any web page. The first page is free so you can see it work; the unlock reads the whole file. PDFs stored on your computer are not supported yet.

If you only want a ruler and never read PDFs, Reader Line is a fine free choice and we say so. If you want the ruler on a toolkit that also bolds word starts and dims everything but the paragraph you are on, or you want it on PDFs, that is ReadFocus. For a broader accessibility suite with read-aloud, see Dyslexly or Helperbird; our reading-extension comparison covers them.

What ReadFocus's ruler does not do yet

  • No colour picker with a hex field — six swatches, three heights and an opacity slider (0.3.0). Tell us if you need more.
  • No PDFs stored on your own disk; PDFs on the web only.
  • No Google Docs (Docs draws its text as graphics, so no extension of this kind can).
