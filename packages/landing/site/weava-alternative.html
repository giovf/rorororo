About Weava

Weava was a good research highlighter, and many people paid for it. Its Chrome listing has not been updated since February 2024, and recent reviews say the same things: highlights are logged but no longer restored on the page, PDF highlighting fails, and Premium subscribers could not find a way to cancel and got no answer from support. If that is you, export your Weava collections before you cancel or delete the account: on the Weava dashboard click the export icon (top right) and choose .csv for each folder. Then in Highlight Keep's library click Import Weava and pick that file — every highlight arrives on its page with its note, its folder as a tag and the nearest of our colours, anchored to its words; nothing already in your library is touched, and importing the same file twice adds nothing. Weava documents what the export holds, not its exact column names, so the importer reads the file's header row; if it cannot find the page address and the highlight text it says which columns it saw instead of guessing, and the first line of that file, sent to info@gankdat.com, is all we need to fix it. Keep a Word export as well — it is the copy you can read without any tool.

About Super Simple Highlighter

Super Simple Highlighter is free, local and, as the name says, simple — a fair choice if that is all you need. The gaps its users report are the ones we built around: saved pages that vanish from the vault, highlights that slide off the text, no notes, no search. Highlight Keep anchors each highlight to the surrounding words and falls back to position only if the text itself has gone, keeps a searchable library, and can back the whole thing up to a file. Moving across is one file: in Super Simple Highlighter open Options → Advanced → Backup, then in Highlight Keep's library click Import Super Simple Highlighter and pick that file. Every highlight arrives on its page, in the nearest of our colours, anchored to its words; nothing already in your library is touched.

About Web Highlights and Marqly

These two are the names that come up when you search for a Weava alternative today, and both are maintained. Web Highlights (200,000 users, 4.8 stars) works offline without an account, which is the right design; what it sells is cloud sync and a web app, as a Premium or Ultimate subscription after a 7-day trial, and its own FAQ warns that importing a backup replaces every highlight you have. Marqly (1,000 users, 3.6 stars) is a bookmark manager with a six-colour highlighter inside it; every highlight lives in your Marqly account, the free plan stops at 100 bookmarks and 10 notes, and Pro is $49 for the first year, then $72 a year. If you want a subscription with sync, Web Highlights is the stronger of the two. If you want your highlights in your own browser with nothing to renew, that is what Highlight Keep is for: free, or $12 once.

What Highlight Keep does not do

  • No highlighting of PDFs stored on your own disk. PDFs on the web work since 0.2.0 (click Highlight this PDF in the popup); local files are not readable by the extension.
  • No sync server. Your highlights stay in the browser profile you made them in; the backup file is how you move them to another machine.
  • Like Super Simple Highlighter, it asks once per site before it runs there. That is what keeps it from reading pages you never asked it to. If you would rather have it on everywhere, one switch in the popup asks once for all sites.
