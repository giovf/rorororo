About Reader Mode

Reader Mode is the first result for its own name and has 100,000 users, which is why so many people land on it. Its listing was last updated in November 2025 and the recent reviews describe the same things: a STATUS_ACCESS_VIOLATION crash in Chrome that stops when the extension is disabled, sites whose buttons stop working while it is installed, a welcome page that never loads, and a lifetime promo bought for a feature that turned out not to exist. If you liked its clean-text view, Chrome's own built-in reader does that part well. What ReadFocus adds is the reading help on the page you are actually on — bolded word starts, a ruler, one paragraph at a time — without replacing the page or crashing it.

About the official bolding extension

The company that trademarked the technique publishes its own Chrome extension. It has 100,000 users and a 2.4-star rating from 316 reviews; the listing has not been updated since June 2024. The pattern in the reviews is not that the idea is bad — the people who got it working love it — but that nothing happens when you click, there are no instructions, it has to be started again on every single page, and it does not work on PDFs or Google Docs. ReadFocus switches on once per site and stays on, handles new text as it loads in feeds and forums, and never touches a text box you are typing in.

About ADHD Reading

ADHD Reading is the polished newcomer: local-first, a free tier with three emphasis modes, and Pro at $29 a year or $59 lifetime for typography controls, per-site auto-apply and profiles. It is a fair product and we would not talk you out of it. The differences are price and shape: ReadFocus gives you per-site auto-apply and three bolding presets for free, and the whole of the rest — precise strength, paragraph focus, the fonts — for $12 once, with a 14-day refund and an address that answers.

About Half Bold and Dyslexly

These are the two names that come up today when you search for an ADHD or dyslexia reading extension, and both are free and both are maintained, so here is the honest version. Half Bold (10,000 users, 4.8 stars) does one thing well: it bolds the start of every word on a page, with the OpenDyslexic font and focus modes on top, and its FAQ says it will stay free. It does not read PDFs (they need converting first) and the bold strength is fixed. Dyslexly (271 users, 5.0 stars from 5 ratings) is a toolkit: fonts, spacing, colour overlays, line focus, a ruler, read-aloud and its own PDF reader, all free and without an account, with a Pro tier for writing and voice help. If either does what you need, use it. What ReadFocus adds is a precise bold strength, paragraph focus, and web PDFs opened as real reflowed text so the bold starts, ruler and fonts work on them, for $12 once rather than a subscription.

If you came here for a reading ruler

The free ruler extensions are small and well liked, and almost every top review of them asks for the same thing: PDF support. ReadFocus's ruler is free too, follows the mouse, toggles with a shortcut and can be combined with the bolding and paragraph focus. Since 0.2.0 it works in PDF files on the web too: "Read this PDF" in the popup opens the file in ReadFocus's own reader as real text, so the ruler, the bolding and the fonts all apply. The first page is free; the unlock reads the whole file. PDFs stored on your computer are not supported yet.

What ReadFocus does not do

  • No PDF files stored on your computer yet (web PDFs work since 0.2.0).
  • No Google Docs. Docs draws its text as graphics rather than page text, so no extension of this kind can bold it; we say so rather than list it as a feature.
  • No text-to-speech. If you want a full suite with a reader voice, Helperbird is a well-maintained one at $30 a year; ReadFocus is the small, one-payment tool.
  • It asks once per site before it runs there. That is what keeps it from reading pages you never asked it to.
